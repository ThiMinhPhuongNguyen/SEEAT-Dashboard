Executive Insights

Key messages from the current Trust overview

CEO VIEW
Trust-wide summary

Prioritise Secondary while keeping Primary improvement targeted

Secondary should be the Trust’s immediate priority, with broader and more interconnected challenges across attendance, persistent absence, behaviour and attainment. The clearest areas for action are SEND attendance and persistent absence, disadvantaged suspensions, and School B, where several concerns converge.

Primary is performing relatively well overall, with most benchmarked measures meeting or outperforming national benchmarks, unlike the broader challenges seen in Secondary. Improvement should therefore remain targeted around disadvantaged persistent absence, Y6 outcomes and SEND attainment / behaviour. School B requires particular attention, where disadvantaged persistent absence and weaker Y6 outcomes coincide, while strong phonics performance should be protected and sustained.

Across both phases, the Trust should use stronger schools as internal comparators and transfer effective practice to schools with greater scope to improve.

PRIMARY

Broadly positive, with targeted opportunities to strengthen outcomes

Targeted improvement

Primary performance is broadly positive, with several clear strengths alongside targeted opportunities to improve. Phonics and GLD are standout strengths, including strong outcomes for disadvantaged and SEND pupils, and overall persistent absence is better than national. However, strong early outcomes are not consistently sustained into Y6, SEND attainment varies substantially between schools, and SEND suspensions are highly concentrated in particular schools.

OBSERVED RESULTS

What the data is showing

Positive outcomes

Phonics is a standout strength

Performance is strong overall and also for disadvantaged and SEND pupils, making this one of the clearest strengths in Primary.

GLD is another clear Primary strength

GLD is above national overall, with particularly strong performance for disadvantaged pupils. The disadvantaged result is around 66% vs 51% national, showing a substantial positive gap.

Overall persistent absence is better than national

At Trust level, overall Primary persistent absence is performing positively against the national benchmark.

School D demonstrates very strong Y6 performance

School D is the strongest internal comparator, including 81% Y6 RWM overall, 80% disadvantaged RWM and 93% Maths.

Areas to strengthen

Disadvantaged persistent absence has clear scope to improve

Several schools are worse than national, with School B at 64% the clearest outlier.

Y6 outcomes show an opportunity to sustain early strengths more consistently

Strong early outcomes, particularly in Phonics and for disadvantaged and SEND pupils, are not consistently sustained to the end of KS2. At Y6, Maths is the weakest Trust-level component, SEND outcomes are weak, and disadvantaged RWM is above national mainly because Schools C and D lift the average while most schools remain below benchmark.

SEND attainment is a wider Primary improvement area

Weak outcomes appear across multiple schools, including School A SEND Y6 RWM at 0% and School F SEND Y4 MTC at 0%.

SEND suspension concentration is especially high in Schools C and E

In both schools, 100% of suspensions involve SEND pupils. The scale differs significantly: School E records 24 of 24, while School C records 4 of 4. This makes School E the stronger priority, while School C still warrants review because of the concentration.

SEND GLD is positive overall, but the Trust result is not consistent across schools

Trust SEND GLD is above national overall, but this is driven mainly by School C at 44%. The other schools with SEND GLD data are below the 21% national benchmark: School D 20% and School E 15%. This means the positive Trust average masks important school-level variation.

FOCUS AREAS

Where leadership action should be concentrated

School B — disadvantaged attendance and Y6 attainment

Focus: reduce disadvantaged persistent absence and strengthen Y6 attainment, with particular attention to Maths.

School A — SEND Y6 attainment

Focus: investigate the factors behind SEND RWM at 0% and identify the support needed to improve end-of-KS2 outcomes.

School E — SEND behaviour and GLD

Focus: review SEND behaviour because 100% of suspensions involve SEND pupils and the volume is high, while also improving SEND GLD (15% vs 21% national). Consider whether the same pupils, needs or provision issues are contributing across both outcomes.

School F — SEND attainment

Focus: strengthen SEND attainment, particularly Y4 MTC and Y6 RWM.

School C — SEND suspension concentration

Focus: review why 100% of suspensions involve SEND pupils, while recognising that the overall volume is low at 4.

Y6 outcomes, progression and disadvantaged attainment

Focus: identify where strong early outcomes are not sustained into KS2, especially in Maths and SEND, and improve consistency in disadvantaged Y6 outcomes by learning from Schools C and D, particularly School D.

SEND attainment across Primary

Focus: treat SEND attainment as a cross-Primary improvement theme, with particular attention to Schools A, E and F.

School D — internal comparator

Focus: identify the teaching, intervention and pupil-support practices behind stronger Y6 outcomes and assess what can transfer to other schools.

GLD — protect strong disadvantaged outcomes

Focus: understand what is supporting strong GLD performance, particularly for disadvantaged pupils, and sustain this strength into later Primary stages.

Phonics — protect and sustain strong practice

Focus: understand what is driving strong overall, disadvantaged and SEND phonics outcomes and sustain that practice into later key stages.

SECONDARY

Broad and interconnected challenges across Secondary

Urgent improvement

Secondary performance shows broad and interconnected challenges across attendance, persistent absence, behaviour and attainment. Attendance is below national across all key cohorts, persistent absence is substantially above national levels, behaviour pressures are significant, and attainment varies widely between schools. The overall picture suggests that Secondary needs a broad improvement response, but the underlying drivers differ by cohort and by school.

OBSERVED RESULTS

What the data is showing

Positive outcomes

School A provides a strong internal comparator

School A has much stronger attainment, lower persistent absence and far fewer suspensions than Schools B and C.

Disadvantaged attendance can perform well

School A disadvantaged attendance is above national and disadvantaged persistent absence is below national, showing that stronger practice already exists within the phase.

Key challenges

Secondary underperforms across most key criteria

The overall Secondary picture is weak across multiple dimensions: attendance is below national across key cohorts, persistent absence is substantially above national, behaviour volumes are high, and attainment varies widely between schools. This indicates a broad phase-level challenge rather than a single isolated issue.

SEND attendance is the clearest phase-wide attendance concern

SEND attendance is materially below national at Trust level and remains low across all three Secondary schools. This is more consistent than the disadvantaged pattern, where School A performs strongly.

Persistent absence is severe across Secondary

Persistent absence is substantially above national overall and affects all key cohorts. SEND is the weakest Trust-level subgroup where data is available, while disadvantaged PA is also high.

Disadvantaged pupils account for the largest share of suspensions

Around 71% of Secondary suspensions involve disadvantaged pupils. The concentration is particularly high in Schools B and C, making disadvantaged behaviour a key subgroup issue.

SEND behaviour is also important, but the pattern differs by school

SEND accounts for a substantial share of suspensions, but the concentration is less consistent than disadvantaged suspensions across the phase.

Attainment shows a very large internal gap

School A is substantially ahead of Schools B and C overall and for disadvantaged pupils. SEND attainment is also low in Schools B and C, although School A SEND attainment data is not supplied.

FOCUS AREAS

Where leadership action should be concentrated

School B — urgent cross-metric priority

Focus: review attendance, behaviour and attainment together; identify repeat pupils, year-group patterns, SEND/disadvantaged overlap, suspension reasons and whether the same cohorts are driving multiple outcomes.

SEND attendance and persistent absence

Focus: treat SEND attendance as a phase-wide improvement priority, investigate SEND persistent absence in Schools B and C, and confirm the missing School A position before drawing a full phase conclusion.

Disadvantaged behaviour

Focus: investigate why disadvantaged pupils account for the majority of Secondary suspensions, particularly in Schools B and C, and test links with persistent absence and attainment.

School C — attendance and attainment

Focus: build a school-specific improvement plan around persistent absence and attainment, recognising that its behaviour profile is less severe than School B.

Disadvantaged attendance and PA — learn from School A

Focus: identify the attendance practice working in School A and assess what can transfer to Schools B and C.

Attainment variation

Focus: understand the drivers of the gap between School A and Schools B/C, including how absence, behaviour, curriculum and intervention may be contributing.

School A — internal comparator

Focus: identify transferable practice in disadvantaged attendance, persistent absence, attainment and wider school systems that may support improvement in Schools B and C.

RECOMMENDATIONS

Leadership priorities

1

Prioritise Secondary improvement. Secondary should be the Trust’s immediate focus because challenges are broader and more interconnected across attendance, persistent absence, behaviour and attainment.

2

Secondary — focus first on SEND attendance / persistent absence and disadvantaged suspensions. Make School B the immediate school-level priority, with School C as the next focused priority for attendance and attainment.

3

Secondary — reduce attainment gaps between schools. Compare practice across schools and use School A as the main internal comparator to identify what can be transferred to Schools B and C.

4

Primary — keep improvement targeted. Focus on disadvantaged persistent absence, Y6 outcomes and SEND attainment / behaviour overall, with particular attention to School B across multiple measures. Also review the most extreme school-level outcomes: School A for SEND Y6 RWM, School E for SEND suspensions, School F for SEND MTC / Y6 RWM, and School C for SEND suspension concentration.

5

Learn from stronger schools and transfer effective practice. Use School D in Primary and School A in Secondary as key internal comparators, with School C also useful for disadvantaged Y6 practice where relevant.

6

Primary — protect and sustain strong phonics practice. Understand what is driving strong overall, disadvantaged and SEND phonics outcomes and sustain that practice into later key stages.
